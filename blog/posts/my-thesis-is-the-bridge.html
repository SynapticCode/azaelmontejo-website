My Thesis is the Bridge: Why I'm Pursuing Both Computer Science and Civil Justice

January 2025 | Academic Journey

People always look a little confused when I say I am studying both Computer Science and Civil Justice. Fair. On paper, they can look like two different planets. But for me, they are the same mission.

Why Both?

Computer Science gives me the tools to build real things. Civil Justice gives me the context for who I am building for and why it matters in the first place. One without the other feels incomplete.

I do not want to build technology that ignores people. I also do not want to study justice as theory only, without the ability to create solutions. I want both the blueprint and the responsibility that comes with it.

What This Looks Like

This dual lens already shapes how I move through opportunities and projects. Last summer, I researched computational cognitive science at Princeton's Velez CoLab, and it was a perfect example of both worlds colliding in a useful way.

The technical side pushed me to think clearly, test ideas, and build better models. The Civil Justice side kept me focused on people, behavior, and outcomes that matter beyond a lab environment.

Where This Goes

I am not trying to become a pure coder or a pure policy person. I am building at the intersection, where technical skill and human understanding actually need each other. That is where the most meaningful work happens.

I want to create experiences and platforms that serve real people, not just perfect demos. If the system works in theory but fails people in practice, it is not done.

The bridge is not just my thesis. It is how I think, how I build, and where I am going.
